The commit graph

The graph is the main view of a repository: every commit, the branches and tags that point at it, and the lines connecting them.

It doesn't load the whole history when you open a repository. It loads the commits just ahead of where you're looking, and more as you scroll, search or jump. That's what keeps it quick on repositories with a million commits or more.

Commits are in date order, newest at the top, so work that happened side by side, such as a gh-pages site and main, is interleaved as it happened. Every commit still sits below the commits built on it.

Your Trunk branch (main, master or develop) runs straight down the left-hand column in rose, and other lines branch off it to the right. A second Trunk branch, such as develop beside main, gets the next column.

Moving around​

  • Scroll, or use the arrow keys to move the selection one commit at a time.
  • Page Up and Page Down move a page at a time.
  • Home and End go to the newest and oldest commits.

Selecting a commit shows its details on the right: author, committer, message, notes, signature and the files it changed. Trailers such as Co-authored-by and Closes are listed on their own, and #14 links to the issue on your repository's host.

Searching​

Press Ctrl+F to search. You can search by commit message, author or SHA. You can also search for a trailer, such as Co-authored-by: Sam or Closes: #12.

Enter goes to the next match and Shift+Enter to the previous one. Matches further back than what's loaded are found too; the graph loads up to them. Find a commit shows it in action.

Branches and tags​

The sidebar has an icon rail and one panel at a time: Branches, Remotes, Tags, Stashes and Repository. Click an icon to switch panels, or click the active one to hide the panel and give the graph the width. Drag the panel's right edge to resize it, and double-click the edge to reset it. Git Revenge remembers all of this for each repository.

Names with a / form folders, so release/v0.8 and release/v0.7 sit in a release folder. Name or Recent, at the top of the panel, sorts by name or by most recently updated. The checked-out branch always comes first, marked with a rose dot and in bold, and other branches are muted. A branch shows one status at a time (parent merged, gone, or how far ahead or behind it is); hover over it for the rest. Hovering or tabbing to a row also shows a … button with the same menu as right-click.

Type in Filter all panels to narrow the lists. The filter stays on as you switch panels, and the rail shows how many matches each panel has. Needs care, in the Branches panel, shows branches whose upstream is gone or whose stack parent was merged. Click a branch or tag to jump to its commit.

The sidebar works from the keyboard. Ctrl+Shift+B jumps to the filter, and ↓ moves from there into the list. In the list, the arrow keys move and open folders, Enter shows a branch in the graph, Space checks it out, the Menu key or Shift+F10 opens its menu, and typing jumps to a name. The rail is one Tab stop, and ↑ and ↓ move along it.

Local branches are grouped by kind: Trunk at the top, then ordinary branches, releases, environments, docs and bots. Branches built on other branches sit under them as a stack. A branch's chip in the graph carries its kind's colour and icon.

Docs and Bots​

When a repository has Docs branches (such as gh-pages) or Bot branches (such as dependabot/…), the graph's header has Docs and Bots buttons to show or hide them. Docs lines are drawn faded either way. Other branches can be hidden too; see hiding branches.

Two layouts​

The default timeline layout gives each commit two lines: the subject, and the author and date. Compact rows fits one commit per line, so you see more history at once. Switch between them in Settings.

Right-click menus​

Right-click a commit, branch or tag for what you can do with it: check it out, create a branch or tag there, merge, rebase, cherry-pick, revert, reset, or see its reflog. Branches, merges and rebases covers these.
